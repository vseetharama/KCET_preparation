BIOLOGY EXAM SAMPLE QUESTIONS

Chapter 1: Cell Structure and Function

Question Set 1: Basic Concepts
1. The Plasma Membrane is a phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and exits the cell.
2. The Nucleus is the membrane-bound organelle that contains the cell's DNA and controls cell activities.
3. Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell, producing ATP through aerobic respiration.
4. Ribosomes are sites of Protein Synthesis where mRNA is translated into proteins.
5. The Endoplasmic Reticulum is a network of membranes involved in protein and lipid synthesis.

Chapter 2: Photosynthesis

Question Set 2: Photosynthesis Process
1. Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert solar energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
2. The Light Reactions occur in the Thylakoid Membranes of the Chloroplast and require sunlight.
3. The Calvin Cycle occurs in the Stroma of the Chloroplast and produces glucose without requiring light.
4. Chlorophyll is the pigment that absorbs light energy during photosynthesis.
5. Photosynthesis equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Chapter 3: Cellular Respiration

Question Set 3: ATP Production
1. Cellular Respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to release energy in the form of ATP.
2. Aerobic Respiration requires Oxygen and produces approximately 36 ATP molecules per glucose molecule.
3. Anaerobic Respiration does not require oxygen and produces only 2 ATP molecules per glucose.
4. The Krebs Cycle, also called the Citric Acid Cycle, is the second stage of aerobic respiration.
5. The Electron Transport Chain is located in the Inner Mitochondrial Membrane and produces the most ATP.

Chapter 4: DNA and Protein Synthesis

Question Set 4: Genetic Information
1. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is the molecule that carries genetic instructions for all living organisms.
2. Transcription is the process where DNA is copied into mRNA in the nucleus.
3. Translation is the process where mRNA is decoded to produce proteins at the ribosome.
4. Codons are sequences of three nucleotides on mRNA that code for specific amino acids.
5. The Genetic Code is nearly universal across all organisms, supporting the theory of evolution.
